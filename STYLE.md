# STYLE.md: deliberate choices. Do not strip.

Owner: Dr Non. Last checked: 2026-10-20.

Everything here was chosen on purpose. Agents and reviewers read this before any design review, never remove or "fix" what it lists, and ask the owner if a choice seems to cause a real problem (contrast, legibility, a broken layout). This file beats every generic rule, including Non-Slop's.

The reference is Braun's control surfaces and civil-aviation operations boards — engraved lettering on a device, hairlines, one orange key. Not a SaaS landing page. The subject is a bus: a real public bus on a real road, and the people who need to know when it arrives.

## Type

- Display: **Josefin Sans**. Geometric, high x-height, drawn-on-a-device feel. Used for the few display moments only.
- Body: **Source Sans 3**. Humanist, warm, legible at 17px on a phone in sunlight. Thai: **IBM Plex Sans Thai** (non-looped — Sarabun is banned). Chinese: Simplified, falls back to the system CJK face.
- **No third family. There is no monospace UI face.** JetBrains Mono was removed 2026-10-19: it set "4 min" in a code-editor face at 44px on the rider's phone and read as a developer console, which is what it is. Figures that must line up use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` in the body face. Do not reintroduce a monospace as a typographic voice — weight and colour do that job.
- A monospace is allowed in exactly one place, via `--font-code` (the platform's face, never a download): `<pre>`/`<code>`, raw tracker JSON, ticket numbers. Braces and indentation have to line up there and nowhere else.

### Where the display face may be used, and at what weight

Josefin Sans is a high-contrast geometric with thin hairlines and closed counters. Set it heavy and below ~40px, the counters fill in and the letters fuse — it reads as a blob, not as a typeface. That blob is the "fat ugly font": it was the rider app's "Next real buses" heading at 25.6px in weight 700, and it was never a font-loading fault. (That heading renders on the rider domain at phone widths; the other routes have their own headings, and the rule below binds all of them.)

- **< 18px — body face only.** Micro-labels, table cells, captions. Source Sans 3, weight 700 for emphasis.
- **18–39px — display face, weight 400 or 500.** Never 600 or above. This is the band that covers section headings and mid-size figures: "Next real buses", the flight board's hour label, the study KPI.
- **≥ 40px — display face, 600 and above allowed.** This is the editorial band: the toolkit's wall-of-type moments (54px and up), the `/ops` hero figure, the capacity screen's 56–72px numbers. The hero figure sits at **400–500**, which is the brand voice: thin, drawn-on, not a poster.
- **All-caps tracked micro-labels are body face, always.** `letter-spacing` ≥ 0.06em on uppercase is template chrome; the label may stay, the shout and the deco face go.
- The family is set on **`html` as well as `body`**. A `body`-level declaration alone leaves `<html>` itself on the UA default — which is Times New Roman. A 16px Times is the "unstyled" signal that makes a product look unfinished.
- **The root's font-size stays where the browser puts it (16px).** `rem` resolves against the root, so a font-size on `html` rescales every measurement in the product at once — the product's 17px lives on `body`. Do not move it up.

Evidence: the type ramp that produced this rule is rendered by `scripts/design/typetransform.mjs`'s sibling, `non-slop-shots/ramp.html` (scratch). The committed instruments are `scripts/design/typeaudit2.mjs`, which inventories the rendered type of **every** route the app serves at **1440 and 390** and fails on any violation of the bands above; `scripts/design/tabaudit.mjs`, which does the same across the toolkit's five tabs, because the hub mounts one panel at a time and a route-level audit only ever sees the first; `scripts/design/capsprobe.mjs`, which counts tracked all-caps labels per route; `scripts/design/interactcheck.mjs`, which checks the ticker, the deep links and the absence of off-palette colour; `scripts/design/headingprobe.mjs`, which lists every heading with the rule that decided its weight; and `scripts/design/removedclasscheck.mjs`, which proves a CSS change removed nothing that renders. Run the audit against the **deployed** build, not against a local preview and not against a URL hard-coded in the script: a type rule that only holds on seven of eighteen routes is not a rule, and a rule only checked at 1440 is not a rule either — four toolkit headings were legal at 1440 and blobs at 390.

**The invariant, for anyone adding a heading:** the base heading weight in the toolkit is 500, and a rule that overrides a heading's `font-size` downward **must declare its own `font-weight`**. It will not inherit the right one.

## Colour

## Layout

- The map is the operating table. It takes the largest share of the first screen and nothing covers it on load. Everything else is an instrument docked at its edge, sized to its content.
- **Empty space is a decision**, never a filler. If a block could be removed without losing meaning, it is removed.
- No box, border, shadow, radius, pill or badge unless it groups things that belong together or it encodes a state.
- The intent is three sizes on a page: display, body, micro. It is not yet true. **Measured 2026-10-20 on the DEPLOYED site at 1440** — distinct rendered font sizes per route: `/research` 3 · `/info` `/more` `/stops` `/ride` `/compare` 5 · `/` 7 · `/driver` 7 · `/pass` 8 · `/fleet` 9 · `/study` 9 · `/ops` 10 · `/v2` 10 · `/roi` 11 · `/governor` 11 · `/capacity` 14 · `/fleet/raw` 15 · `/toolkit` 27. **Method, so the number can be reproduced and compared:** only elements that own a non-whitespace text node count, because an element that merely contains text inherits its parent's size and would double-count one physical size; sizes are compared at 0.5px. **Measure it on the deployed site, not on a local preview** — a preview has no backend, renders fewer elements and reports fewer sizes, which is exactly what the first version of this line did. A live page can also render one more size than the number above when the feed returns a state that draws an extra element, so `/fleet` reads 9 or 10. And the 390 pass is not optional: four toolkit headings were legal at 1440 and blobs at 390. The rule is ≤8. Treat a fourth size on a page as a decision to justify, and shrink the ramp before adding to it. This line records the intent and the measured state; the gap between them is the type-scale scanner hit, and it is not closed.
- **One radius for the product.** The house radius is 0 (`--radius-sm/-md/-lg/-pill` are all 0). Full round is reserved for true circles and pills (`50%`, `999px`). One shape keeps a corner radius of its own: `20px 20px 0 0`, a photo with two rounded top corners. Nothing else gets a radius.

## The depa · USDOT toolkit (this branch)

The toolkit is a research surface for a government partner, a lender and a city. It is not the bus app, and it is not a landing page. Its house rules are its own, and they are deliberate:

- **Paper `#f5f5f2`, ink `#111820`, signal red `#ef442f`.** Red is the one signal colour: it marks the claim, the rule, the warning. `--tk-blue` and `--tk-yellow` are structural tones (a table header, a photo caption strip), not competing accents. The branch's own comment in `toolkit-showcase.css` says it: one signal red, hard rules, real photographs, no card confetti.
- **Hairline table grids, not boxes.** The toolkit's data tables are ruled with a 1px ink line. No card, no shadow, no radius on a data row.
- **Its own editorial type ramp.** The toolkit sets large display type in the display face at 60–190px — that is the editorial band, and it is why `src/components/toolkit/*.css` is exempt from the ≤8-size rule above. Below 40px the display face still holds the same limit as everywhere else in this product: weight 400 or 500, never 600+.
- **`public/toolkit-source.pdf` is 5.8 MB.** It is a document behind an explicit "Download the toolkit" link, not page weight, and it is the source document the research cites. It is the one heavy file left in `public/` and it is there on purpose.
- **The research photographs live in `assets-src/`, not `public/`.** They are the evidence base and they are versioned in the repository; they are not page assets. Shipping 44 MB of unreferenced photographs to the open web was the single largest piece of dead weight this branch carried.
- **The live half and the modelled half never share a label.** The corner ticker reads the real fleet from the operator's tracker in the "Live · PKSB tracker" block and this page's own model in the "Simulated · this page's model" block. Before 2026-10-20 the badge showed only the model under a "Live" kicker, which is a lie a reader can act on.

## What the branch is, operationally

- The toolkit deploys as a **preview deployment** of the `phuket-smart-bus` Pages project. `bus.nonarkara.org` is the **production branch** of the same project. The two never touch: `scripts/deploy-usdot.sh` deploys with `--branch=depa-usdot.nonarkara.org` and cannot overwrite production.
- **Pages Functions only run on the production branch.** So `cloudflare/toolkit-domain-router.ts` — the Worker that holds the `depa-usdot.nonarkara.org` custom domain — routes `/api/*` to the production deployment and everything else to the toolkit's preview deployment. That split is why the published domain can serve the real tracker relay and the research archive at all.
- **Test-data injection does not exist in a production build.** The DEVICES console's "Inject Live GPS Pulse" fabricates positions for real PKSB plate numbers; on a dev machine that is a fixture, on a published wall it is a lie. It is behind `import.meta.env.DEV`.
- The source register (`src/components/toolkit/DataProvenance.tsx`) carries a **"Live"** freshness value that means the operator's tracker is reporting it now. A published timetable is **"Published"**, not "Live" — the legend says so, and so does the register.
