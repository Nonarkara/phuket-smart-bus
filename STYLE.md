# STYLE.md: deliberate choices. Do not strip.

Owner: Dr Non. Last checked: 2026-10-20.

Everything here was chosen on purpose. Agents and reviewers read this before any design review, never remove or "fix" what it lists, and ask the owner if a choice seems to cause a real problem (contrast, legibility, a broken layout). This file beats every generic rule, including Non-Slop's.

The reference is Braun's control surfaces and civil-aviation operations boards — engraved lettering on a device, hairlines, one orange key. Not a SaaS landing page. The subject is a bus: a real public bus on a real road, and the people who need to know when it arrives.

## Type

- Display: **Josefin Sans**. Geometric, high x-height, drawn-on-a-device feel. Used for the few display moments only.
- Body: **Source Sans 3**. Humanist, warm, legible at 17px on a phone in sunlight. Thai: **IBM Plex Sans Thai** (non-looped — Sarabun is banned). Chinese: Simplified, falls back to the system CJK face.
- **No third family. There is no monospace UI face.** JetBrains Mono was removed 2026-10-19: it set "4 min" in a code-editor face at 44px on the rider's phone and read as a developer console, which is what it is. Figures that must line up use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` in the body face. Do not reintroduce a monospace as a typographic voice — weight and colour do that job.
- A monospace is allowed in exactly one place, via `--font-code` (the platform's face, never a download): `<pre>`/`<code>`, raw tracker JSON, ticket numbers. Braces and indentation have to line up there and nowhere else.

### Where the display face may be used, and at what weight

Josefin Sans is a high-contrast geometric with thin hairlines and closed counters. Set it heavy and below ~40px, the counters fill in and the letters fuse — it reads as a blob, not as a typeface. That blob is the "fat ugly font": it was the rider app's "Next real buses" heading at 25.6px in weight 700, and it was never a font-loading fault. (That heading renders on the rider domain at phone widths; the other routes have their own headings, and the rule below binds all of them.)

- **< 18px — body face only.** Micro-labels, table cells, captions. Source Sans 3, weight 700 for emphasis.
- **18–39px — display face, weight 400 or 500.** Never 600 or above. This is the band that covers section headings and mid-size figures: "Next real buses", the flight board's hour label, the study KPI.
- **≥ 40px — display face, 600 and above allowed.** This is the editorial band: the toolkit's wall-of-type moments (54px and up), the `/ops` hero figure, the capacity screen's 56–72px numbers. The hero figure sits at **400–500**, which is the brand voice: thin, drawn-on, not a poster.
- **All-caps tracked micro-labels are body face, always.** `letter-spacing` ≥ 0.06em on uppercase is template chrome; the label may stay, the shout and the deco face go.
- The family is set on **`html` as well as `body`**. A `body`-level declaration alone leaves `<html>` itself on the UA default — which is Times New Roman. A 16px Times is the "unstyled" signal that makes a product look unfinished.
- **The root's font-size stays where the browser puts it (16px).** `rem` resolves against the root, so a font-size on `html` rescales every measurement in the product at once — the product's 17px lives on `body`. Do not move it up.

Evidence: the type ramp that produced this rule is rendered by `scripts/design/typetransform.mjs`'s sibling, `non-slop-shots/ramp.html` (scratch). The committed instruments are `scripts/design/typeaudit2.mjs`, which inventories the rendered type of **every** route the app serves at **1440 and 390** and fails on any violation of the bands above; `scripts/design/tabaudit.mjs`, which does the same across the toolkit's five tabs, because the hub mounts one panel at a time and a route-level audit only ever sees the first; `scripts/design/capsprobe.mjs`, which counts tracked all-caps labels per route; `scripts/design/interactcheck.mjs`, which checks the ticker, the deep links and the absence of off-palette colour; `scripts/design/headingprobe.mjs`, which lists every heading with the rule that decided its weight; and `scripts/design/removedclasscheck.mjs`, which proves a CSS change removed nothing that renders. Run the audit against the **deployed** build, not against a local preview and not against a URL hard-coded in the script: a type rule that only holds on seven of eighteen routes is not a rule, and a rule only checked at 1440 is not a rule either — four toolkit headings were legal at 1440 and blobs at 390.

**The invariant, for anyone adding a heading:** the base heading weight in the toolkit is 500, and a rule that overrides a heading's `font-size` downward **must declare its own `font-weight`**. It will not inherit the right one.

**The editorial surfaces set their headings in the DISPLAY face, at 400–500.** The hero and the chapter titles of the toolkit and the insights bridge are Josefin Sans; the body copy is Source Sans 3. A heavy grotesque is not a substitute for the geometric: the toolkit's hero once read "We Did the Research. Then We Made It Move." in the body face at weight 800 — that is the "fat ugly front" this whole section was written about, and it was invisible for as long as the rule that should have set the display face was a dead declaration. Weight 600 and above in the display face is legal only at 40px and up, and even there the brand voice is thin: 400–500, drawn on, not a poster.

### The type scale — eight names, one per surface

The law is **≤8 distinct rendered font sizes on a page**. It was open, and the measured state was bad: the toolkit's `build` tab rendered 36 sizes on its own, the fleet detail 15, the capacity screen 14, and `/ops` ten — several of them 9–11px microcopy, below the floor the ops wall claims for itself.

Eight names, shared by every surface. **The names are the vocabulary; the values are per surface**, because an operations board at 50 inches and an editorial publication are not the same object and should not pretend to be:

| Token | What it is |
|---|---|
| `--t-display` | the biggest thing on the surface — the hero figure, the display title |
| `--t-hero` | the second display level, below `--t-display` |
| `--t-figure` | a large data number |
| `--t-title` | a heading |
| `--t-lead` | a standfirst or lead-in |
| `--t-body` | body copy |
| `--t-label` | a label or caption |
| `--t-micro` | the smallest text |

A surface declares its own **values** for these names in one block, and every `font-size` in that surface's CSS uses one of them. A surface that uses six of the eight has six sizes; the rule is satisfied by construction, not by vigilance.

**Why tokens and not fewer literals.** A literal `font-size` is a size nobody can reason about: the ramp was 37 distinct values in `src/styles.css` alone, and no two of them were the same *role*. A token carries the role with it, so the next person to add a number adds it to the scale or does not add it. `:root` already carried `--t-display`, `--t-body` and `--t-micro`; 115 declarations used them and 676 did not. That is the whole defect in one sentence.

**The mapping is monotonic.** A bigger size never maps to a smaller step than a smaller size does. Collapsing a ramp by taking "the nearest value" inverts hierarchy — a 72px hero and a 56px heading both become "the display step" and the page loses the thing it was trying to say. Merge downwards: the band keeps the top of the band.

**Instruments.** `scripts/design/typemap.mjs` maps every `font-size` declaration in a stylesheet to the size it *renders* at (px and rem directly, `clamp()` evaluated at the viewport, `em` reported as parent-relative because a parent's size change moves the child). `scripts/design/scalemap.mjs` measures the rendered result per route and names the elements at each size. Run both against the **deployed** build. A `clamp()` is one size at any width, so a fluid step does not break the count — but it must land on a value that is not one of the fixed steps, or two steps collide and the count drops (which is fine) rather than gaining a ninth (which is not).

**A rule that names a token its scope does not define is a DEAD RULE, and it fails silently.** This is the failure the whole scale was built to prevent, and it shipped: the toolkit view's scope defined three of the eight names, so sixteen declarations — `var(--t-hero)`, `var(--t-label)`, `var(--t-title)`, `var(--t-figure)`, `var(--t-lead)` — were **invalid at computed-value time**. The property inherited instead. The page rendered every text element at 13, 15 or 28px, the hierarchy it had been designed with never appeared, and **nothing failed**: a `var()` with no value is not an error, it is a declaration that quietly does nothing. The hero was set in the body face at weight 800 — the "fat ugly front" — because the rule that would have set the display face was the dead one.

Two rules follow, and they are not optional:

- **Every surface that renders type declares all eight names.** A surface may use six of them; it may not leave one undefined and reference it. A scale block that is missing a name is a trap, not a shortcut.
- **A token is a promise about a scope.** `--t-hero` means the same size everywhere it is defined, but it only means anything at all where it is defined. The scope is part of the token.

`scripts/design/tokenscope.mjs` is the check: for every rule that names a token, the token must actually **resolve** on an element the rule matches. It found 17 dead declarations on the build that had the bug, and is clean on the build that does not. It has two passes that need no browser, and both run in the pre-commit hook: every `--t-*` name referenced must be defined somewhere, and **every scope that defines tokens must define all eight**. The second one is the law above, made mechanical — a scope with a hole in it is a trap. It found three: `:root` declared three of the eight names, `.v2--operations.fc` seven, `.v2--operations.study` seven. All three declare all eight now.

`scripts/design/offscale.mjs` is the other half: every size a page **renders** must be a size the scale **names**. It resolves tokens by *usage* — the sizes that token-ruled elements actually render at — so a fluid step is measured at the size it renders rather than at the floor of its `clamp()`. The first version of this probe parsed the token declarations instead, and cried wolf on the toolkit's 108px hero; an instrument that cries wolf is as useless as one that cannot fail.

**A green light on a check that checked nothing is the worst outcome a gate can have**, because it looks like evidence. Two of these probes were blind for a week, and both were blind in the same direction: they reported "clean" on pages that were wrong.

- **Chrome erases the declaration when it is written as a shorthand.** `font: 500 var(--t-hero)/1.05 var(--font-display)` is stored with every longhand carrying a pending substitution, so `rule.style.fontSize` reads `""`, `rule.style.font` reads `""`, and the property list shows `font-size = ` — present, empty. A rule that names a token looks like a rule that names no size at all. This codebase writes its type in the shorthand form everywhere, so both probes were blind to most of the stylesheet. The fix is not to re-implement the cascade — the browser does that correctly. The fix is to read the **signal**: a rule that lists a `font-size` property whose value reads empty is a rule whose size came from a `var()`. And where the token's *name* is needed, read the stylesheet **text** (`scripts/design/cssrules.mjs`), because that is the only place the name still exists.
- **A selector LIST is not one selector.** `button, input, a` is three selectors of specificity (0,0,1) each. Reading it as one selector and summing its type counts made it look stronger than it is, which changed which rule won, and so which leak was found.
- **A gate must pass its arguments as arguments.** The gate passed the routes to `offscale.mjs` as `--fleet/raw --fleet --study` — flags — and the probe drops anything that starts with `--`. It received zero routes, printed "total off-scale elements: 0", and the gate printed "ok" for a check that had never run.

**The law is enforced, not just recorded.** `scripts/design/typeaudit2.mjs` fails a page that renders more than eight sizes, at 1440 and at 390.

**The exception is closed (2026-10-21).** The rider phone app was the one named, dated exception, and it was the worst offender in the product: **18 sizes at 390**, from 26 distinct `font-size` declarations in `PassengerApp.css` written in `rem` against a 16px root. The screen a person holds in daylight carried microcopy at 9.9px and 10px, and its reading band was a fog of 12.5 / 13.1 / 13.6 / 14.1 / 14.7 / 15.2 — six sizes that are visually one size, which is the yellow-pages flatness this law exists to stop, only sideways.

The sheet now declares the eight names once, on the two roots that can carry them (`.pa-app` for the phone, `.pa-shell` for the portal chrome beside it), and every one of its 83 declarations is one of them. Measured on the rider domain at 390: **18 → 7**.

Three things the pass earned, worth keeping:

- **The reading band is ONE step on purpose.** A standfirst and a paragraph are the same size on a phone in sunlight; a 1px step between them is a fake hierarchy. The standfirst and the body are both 17, the labels are 13, and the smallest text on the screen is 11.
- **A third party can push a surface over the law.** Leaflet ships its own 18px zoom glyph and 12px attribution and tooltip. Neither was the app's, and neither was on the scale — the surface had to name them, or the count would drift back up with no one knowing which size did it.
- **A surface that renders type declares all eight names, on every root that can carry them.** The phone app renders *without* `.pa-shell` (the router returns the app content directly), so a scale declared on `.pa-shell` alone would have been undefined on the phone — the dead-rule trap, in a new shape.

With the exception closed, the switch that turned it off went with it: a switch with nothing to switch is a trap, and a named exception that is closed is a deleted law. The check still bites on any surface that crosses eight.

## Layout

- The map is the operating table. It takes the largest share of the first screen and nothing covers it on load. Everything else is an instrument docked at its edge, sized to its content.
- **Empty space is a decision**, never a filler. If a block could be removed without losing meaning, it is removed.
- No box, border, shadow, radius, pill or badge unless it groups things that belong together or it encodes a state.
- **One page, one left edge.** A page made of stacked bands — a header, a source switcher, a sub-nav, a body — puts every band's **content** on the same line. The bands themselves may be full-bleed; their content may not. This shipped broken: the toolkit's header sat at 24px, its body at 104px, and the top of the page looked like it had been built by a different team than the page under it. The console now has ONE column (`--v2-column`, `--v2-gutter`) and every band reads it; the dark operations wall is a different object and sets `--v2-column: 100%`, so one rule serves both views and they cannot drift apart. `scripts/design/guttercheck.mjs` measures the bands' content edges and fails when they disagree — it reported 80px of disagreement on the build that had the bug, and passes on the build that does not.
- **A box that holds one sentence is a box drawn to fill a gap.** Eight bordered cards, each carrying a short quote, is a yellow pages, not a system. An index you can click and one panel that answers is half the boxes and a page you can use. The same rule kills the dead space under short cards: a grid that stretches every cell to the tallest one is padding the short ones with emptiness.
- **A grid track must be allowed to shrink.** `1fr` is `minmax(auto, 1fr)`, and its automatic minimum is the cell's min-content. Four cells whose text cannot break therefore add up to a track wider than the phone, and everything beside them is pushed off the screen: the toolkit's HUD pipeline went from two columns to four with no responsive override, and on every phone ≤441px the hero's prose was clipped at the right edge — the reader could not read the last characters of every line, and nothing said so. The tracks are `minmax(0, 1fr)`, the cells carry `min-width: 0`, and the pipeline is two columns on a phone. Eleven grids in the toolkit region had the same trap. **A grid is not a layout until it has been measured at 390.**
- **The type ramp is closed on every surface except the rider's phone.** **Measured 2026-10-20 on the DEPLOYED site, 1440 and 390**, with `scripts/design/typeaudit2.mjs` (0 band violations on every route at both widths): at 1440 — `/research` 3 · `/study` 4 · `/info` `/more` `/stops` `/ride` `/compare` 5 · `/` `/ops` `/fleet` `/v2` `/roi` `/driver` 7 · `/fleet/raw` `/capacity` `/toolkit` `/governor` `/pass` 8. At 390 — `/research` 3 · `/study` 4 · `/ops` `/v2` `/info` `/more` `/stops` `/ride` `/compare` 5 · `/driver` 6 · `/capacity` `/fleet` `/toolkit` `/roi` 7 · `/fleet/raw` `/governor` `/pass` 8 · **`/` 16**. **Method, so the number can be reproduced and compared:** only elements that own a non-whitespace text node count, because an element that merely contains text inherits its parent's size and would double-count one physical size; sizes are compared at 0.5px. **Measure it on the deployed site, not on a local preview** — a preview has no backend, renders fewer elements and reports fewer sizes, which is exactly what the first version of this line did. A live page can also render one more size than the number above when the feed returns a state that draws an extra element, so `/fleet` reads 9 or 10. And the 390 pass is not optional: four toolkit headings were legal at 1440 and blobs at 390. The rule is ≤8.
  - **The one that is still open is the rider's phone app**, and it is open on purpose, not by accident. It renders 16 sizes at 390 because it is built in `rem` against a 16px root with about sixty separate `font-size` declarations — `0.62rem` to `2.4rem` — and every one of them lands on its own step. **It does not read as a ramp**: the phone screen shows a clear hierarchy, the display-face heading is a legal 38px, and the audit reports 0 band violations. The count is over the limit the number states; the page is not over the limit the limit is for. Closing it is a deliberate type redesign of the rider UI — a phone-first, six-language surface — and it wants its own reviewed pass, not a drive-by collapse at the end of a long one. It is named here so it cannot be forgotten.
- **One radius for the product.** The house radius is 0 (`--radius-sm/-md/-lg/-pill` are all 0). Full round is reserved for true circles and pills (`50%`, `999px`). One shape keeps a corner radius of its own: `20px 20px 0 0`, a photo with two rounded top corners. Nothing else gets a radius.

## Spatial hallucination

**A layout that invents a reason for things to occupy space, instead of arranging them around how people read, choose and act.** It looks composed at first glance and cannot explain its own geometry. The recurring test is: **"Why is this here, why this size, and why this far from that?"** If the answer is "the grid put it there", the layout has failed. Whitespace is intentional when it separates meaningful groups, supports reading, or establishes emphasis. It becomes hallucination when it asks the reader to supply an intention the designer never had.

Six failure modes, and every one of them is measurable:

1. **Unjustified gaps** — space stretched to fill a container. A grid stretches its cells to the tallest one; the short cell then carries a cushion of nothing.
2. **Orphan elements** — an element stranded in a mostly-empty row. A photo beside an empty area; a 60px span alone in a 588px row.
3. **Scattered actions** — related buttons on different rows, or pushed to opposite edges of a row that is mostly empty.
4. **False hierarchy** — everything gets equal weight, so nothing helps the reader choose. A grid of four or more identical boxed cells that owns half the screen.
5. **Accidental wrapping** — a row breaks where it runs out of room and strands one item alone on the last line.
6. **Spatially expensive information** — a small decision requires a long scroll. A region inside the page that is several screens deep.

**`scripts/design/space.mjs` measures all six, plus content clipped by its own box and scroll traps. It is a screen, not a judge: it reports geometry with its measurements, and a human decides whether the geometry is a decision.**

```bash
node scripts/design/space.mjs "/v2?view=toolkit" /ops --base http://127.0.0.1:4173
```

**It is proven to fail, and proven to be quiet.** `scripts/design/prove-space.sh` serves a fixture carrying one deliberate instance of every mode beside a clean control, and fails if a mode goes uncaught or the control goes noisy. The fixture found four real blindnesses in the instrument itself: a rule that skipped every grid whose children were explicitly placed (so a row that ended with a stranded item went unseen), a rule that compared every pair of actions in a row (so a navigation row spread across the width read as scattered actions), a dead-space rule that judged the page's own body, and an equal-weight rule that fired on every metadata row. A mutation test — blinding one rule — is what proves the proof itself bites.

**The instrument's own false positives are recorded here, because a gate that cries wolf is as useless as one that cannot fail:**

- A ROW is a line of items, not a set of identical tops. A pill is vertically centred and a chip beside it is not; a 3px difference once split one flex line into two, so a pill next to its own chips read as "alone on the last line". Rows are clustered with a tolerance.
- The page's OWN scroll is not a trap. A long briefing on a phone is a long briefing. A region INSIDE the page that is several screens deep is the trap.
- A wide DATA TABLE on a phone is a legitimate horizontal scroll. The map engine sizes its own overlay to the route's bounds, not to the viewport; both are exempt.
- A strip of three parallel facts is not a choice. The reader reads all of it and equal weight is the point. Only a grid of four or more identical cells that owns a large share of the screen is a false hierarchy.
- The page's TAIL is not a hole. Content that ends in the middle of the last screen is the end of the page.
- A cell's mark is the SET of colours it carries, not the colour of its first line. A green figure under a grey label marks the cell as surely as a tinted ground would.

**What the massacre found, measured on the local build at 1440 and 390, 2026-10-21** — the operations wall's right rail was 527px tall and held 1,652px of content: the hour-by-hour analysis sat BETWEEN the headline and the fleet table, so the thing the rail exists for finished 1,482px below the fold and nothing on the surface said it was down there. The fleet table's height came from four rules written when the table sat under the map and the two divided a vertical column; the table moved into the rail, the rules stayed, and the height of the thing the rail exists for was the residue of their source order. The rail was 380px, which the note beside it already said was too narrow, so the ETA column was off the edge and the operator had to scroll a wall sideways to read it. A FULL badge shared the load cell with the bar and squeezed the bar to zero width, so every row's occupancy was invisible. The conditions strip was capped at 110px while its four tiles each needed 230px, so the cap was cutting the tiles, not the strip. On a phone, the briefing's second column wanted 320px, the RED FLAG banner was `width: max-content` — 627px of text in a 388px map — and the timebar's chips and controls together wanted 472px of a 340px row.

**The rules that came out of it, each one earned by a measurement:**

- **A rail's width is set by what the rail holds.** 380px starved both the fleet table's five tracks and the operating equation's three terms. The rail is now `clamp(460px, 34vw, 520px)`, and the map keeps 60%+ at every width.
- **A region's height is a decision, made where the region lives.** Rules left over from a layout that no longer exists will decide the geometry of the one that does, if nobody deletes them.
- **The first screen answers three questions, in order, and nothing else.** How much did we lose today, what do I do about it right now, which bus is it. Everything else is one bounded scroll away.
- **A bar that is squeezed to zero width is a measurement failure, not a design.** The badge next to it was the winner; the bar was the truth.
- **A cap must be applied to the thing that overflows, not to its container.** Capping a strip does not shorten its tiles.
- **A narrow-width rule that sits before the rule it overrides does nothing.** The declaration is there; it simply loses on source order. The narrow-width fixes live at the END of the stylesheet.
- **A peer set must have a leader.** Four or more identical boxed cells that own half the screen are a yellow pages, not a system. Three parallel facts in a row are not a choice, and equal weight is the point.

## The depa · USDOT toolkit (this branch)

The toolkit is a research surface for a government partner, a lender and a city. It is not the bus app, and it is not a landing page. Its house rules are its own, and they are deliberate:

- **Paper `#f5f5f2`, ink `#111820`, signal red `#ef442f`.** Red is the one signal colour: it marks the claim, the rule, the warning. `--tk-blue` and `--tk-yellow` are structural tones (a table header, a photo caption strip), not competing accents. The branch's own comment in `toolkit-showcase.css` says it: one signal red, hard rules, real photographs, no card confetti.
- **Hairline table grids, not boxes.** The toolkit's data tables are ruled with a 1px ink line. No card, no shadow, no radius on a data row.
- **Its own editorial type ramp.** The toolkit sets large display type in the display face at 60–190px — that is the editorial band, and it is why `src/components/toolkit/*.css` is exempt from the ≤8-size rule above. Below 40px the display face still holds the same limit as everywhere else in this product: weight 400 or 500, never 600+.
- **`public/toolkit-source.pdf` is 5.8 MB.** It is a document behind an explicit "Download the toolkit" link, not page weight, and it is the source document the research cites. It is the one heavy file left in `public/` and it is there on purpose.
- **The research photographs live in `assets-src/`, not `public/`.** They are the evidence base and they are versioned in the repository; they are not page assets. Shipping 44 MB of unreferenced photographs to the open web was the single largest piece of dead weight this branch carried.
- **The live half and the modelled half never share a label.** The corner ticker reads the real fleet from the operator's tracker in the "Live · PKSB tracker" block and this page's own model in the "Simulated · this page's model" block. Before 2026-10-20 the badge showed only the model under a "Live" kicker, which is a lie a reader can act on.

## What the branch is, operationally

- The toolkit deploys as a **preview deployment** of the `phuket-smart-bus` Pages project. `bus.nonarkara.org` is the **production branch** of the same project. The two never touch: `scripts/deploy-usdot.sh` deploys with `--branch=depa-usdot.nonarkara.org` and cannot overwrite production.
- **Pages Functions only run on the production branch.** So `cloudflare/toolkit-domain-router.ts` — the Worker that holds the `depa-usdot.nonarkara.org` custom domain — routes `/api/*` to the production deployment and everything else to the toolkit's preview deployment. That split is why the published domain can serve the real tracker relay and the research archive at all.
- **Test-data injection does not exist in a production build.** The DEVICES console's "Inject Live GPS Pulse" fabricates positions for real PKSB plate numbers; on a dev machine that is a fixture, on a published wall it is a lie. It is behind `import.meta.env.DEV`.
- The source register (`src/components/toolkit/DataProvenance.tsx`) carries a **"Live"** freshness value that means the operator's tracker is reporting it now. A published timetable is **"Published"**, not "Live" — the legend says so, and so does the register.
