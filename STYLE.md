# STYLE.md: deliberate choices. Do not strip.

Owner: Dr Non. Last checked: 2026-10-20.

Everything here was chosen on purpose. Agents and reviewers read this before any design review, never remove or "fix" what it lists, and ask the owner if a choice seems to cause a real problem (contrast, legibility, a broken layout). This file beats every generic rule, including Non-Slop's.

The reference is Braun's control surfaces and civil-aviation operations boards — engraved lettering on a device, hairlines, one orange key. Not a SaaS landing page. The subject is a bus: a real public bus on a real road, and the people who need to know when it arrives.

## Type

- Display: **Public Sans** (official USWDS federal transit standard, clean, authoritative civic voice, zero AI-template quirks; Josefin Sans kept as legacy alias).
- Body: **Source Sans 3**. Humanist, warm, legible at 17px on a phone in sunlight. Thai: **IBM Plex Sans Thai** (non-looped — Sarabun is banned). Chinese: Simplified, falls back to the system CJK face.
- **No third family. There is no monospace UI face.** JetBrains Mono was removed 2026-10-19: it set "4 min" in a code-editor face at 44px on the rider's phone and read as a developer console, which is what it is. Figures that must line up use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` in the body face. Do not reintroduce a monospace as a typographic voice — weight and colour do that job.
- A monospace is allowed in exactly one place, via `--font-code` (the platform's face, never a download): `<pre>`/`<code>`, raw tracker JSON, ticket numbers. Braces and indentation have to line up there and nowhere else.

### Where the display face may be used, and at what weight

Josefin Sans is a high-contrast geometric with thin hairlines and closed counters. Set it heavy and below ~40px, the counters fill in and the letters fuse — it reads as a blob, not as a typeface. That blob is the "fat ugly font": it was the rider app's "Next real buses" heading at 25.6px in weight 700, and it was never a font-loading fault. (That heading renders on the rider domain at phone widths; the other routes have their own headings, and the rule below binds all of them.)

- **< 18px — body face only.** Micro-labels, table cells, captions. Source Sans 3, weight 700 for emphasis.
- **18–39px — display face, weight 400 or 500.** Never 600 or above. This is the band that covers section headings and mid-size figures: "Next real buses", the flight board's hour label, the study KPI.
- **≥ 40px — display face, 600 and above allowed.** This is the editorial band: the toolkit's wall-of-type moments (54px and up), the `/ops` hero figure, the capacity screen's 56–72px numbers. The hero figure sits at **400–500**, which is the brand voice: thin, drawn-on, not a poster.
- **All-caps tracked micro-labels are body face, always.** `letter-spacing` ≥ 0.06em on uppercase is template chrome; the label may stay, the shout and the deco face go.
- The family is set on **`html` as well as `body`**. A `body`-level declaration alone leaves `<html>` itself on the UA default — which is Times New Roman. A 16px Times is the "unstyled" signal that makes a product look unfinished.
- **The root's font-size stays where the browser puts it (16px).** `rem` resolves against the root, so a font-size on `html` rescales every measurement in the product at once — the product's 17px lives on `body`. Do not move it up.

Evidence: the type ramp that produced this rule is rendered by `scripts/design/typetransform.mjs`'s sibling, `non-slop-shots/ramp.html` (scratch). The committed instruments are `scripts/design/typeaudit2.mjs`, which inventories the rendered type of **every** route the app serves at **1440 and 390** and fails on any violation of the bands above; `scripts/design/tabaudit.mjs`, which does the same across the toolkit's five tabs, because the hub mounts one panel at a time and a route-level audit only ever sees the first; `scripts/design/capsprobe.mjs`, which counts tracked all-caps labels per route; `scripts/design/interactcheck.mjs`, which checks the ticker, the deep links and the absence of off-palette colour; `scripts/design/headingprobe.mjs`, which lists every heading with the rule that decided its weight; and `scripts/design/removedclasscheck.mjs`, which proves a CSS change removed nothing that renders. Run the audit against the **deployed** build, not against a local preview and not against a URL hard-coded in the script: a type rule that only holds on seven of twenty-four routes is not a rule, and a rule only checked at 1440 is not a rule either — four toolkit headings were legal at 1440 and blobs at 390.

**The invariant, for anyone adding a heading:** the base heading weight in the toolkit is 500, and a rule that overrides a heading's `font-size` downward **must declare its own `font-weight`**. It will not inherit the right one.

**The editorial surfaces set their headings in the DISPLAY face, at 400–500.** The hero and the chapter titles of the toolkit and the insights bridge are Josefin Sans; the body copy is Source Sans 3. A heavy grotesque is not a substitute for the geometric: the toolkit's hero once read "We Did the Research. Then We Made It Move." in the body face at weight 800 — that is the "fat ugly front" this whole section was written about, and it was invisible for as long as the rule that should have set the display face was a dead declaration. Weight 600 and above in the display face is legal only at 40px and up, and even there the brand voice is thin: 400–500, drawn on, not a poster.

### The type scale — eight names, one per surface

The law is **≤8 distinct rendered font sizes on a page**. It was open, and the measured state was bad: the toolkit's `build` tab rendered 36 sizes on its own, the fleet detail 15, the capacity screen 14, and `/ops` ten — several of them 9–11px microcopy, below the floor the ops wall claims for itself.

Eight names, shared by every surface. **The names are the vocabulary; the values are per surface**, because an operations board at 50 inches and an editorial publication are not the same object and should not pretend to be:

| Token | What it is |
|---|---|
| `--t-display` | the biggest thing on the surface — the hero figure, the display title |
| `--t-hero` | the second display level, below `--t-display` |
| `--t-figure` | a large data number |
| `--t-title` | a heading |
| `--t-lead` | a standfirst or lead-in |
| `--t-body` | body copy |
| `--t-label` | a label or caption |
| `--t-micro` | the smallest text |

A surface declares its own **values** for these names in one block, and every `font-size` in that surface's CSS uses one of them. A surface that uses six of the eight has six sizes; the rule is satisfied by construction, not by vigilance.

**Why tokens and not fewer literals.** A literal `font-size` is a size nobody can reason about: the ramp was 37 distinct values in `src/styles.css` alone, and no two of them were the same *role*. A token carries the role with it, so the next person to add a number adds it to the scale or does not add it. `:root` already carried `--t-display`, `--t-body` and `--t-micro`; 115 declarations used them and 676 did not. That is the whole defect in one sentence.

**The mapping is monotonic.** A bigger size never maps to a smaller step than a smaller size does. Collapsing a ramp by taking "the nearest value" inverts hierarchy — a 72px hero and a 56px heading both become "the display step" and the page loses the thing it was trying to say. Merge downwards: the band keeps the top of the band.

**Instruments.** `scripts/design/typemap.mjs` maps every `font-size` declaration in a stylesheet to the size it *renders* at (px and rem directly, `clamp()` evaluated at the viewport, `em` reported as parent-relative because a parent's size change moves the child). `scripts/design/scalemap.mjs` measures the rendered result per route and names the elements at each size. Run both against the **deployed** build. A `clamp()` is one size at any width, so a fluid step does not break the count — but it must land on a value that is not one of the fixed steps, or two steps collide and the count drops (which is fine) rather than gaining a ninth (which is not).

**A rule that names a token its scope does not define is a DEAD RULE, and it fails silently.** This is the failure the whole scale was built to prevent, and it shipped: the toolkit view's scope defined three of the eight names, so sixteen declarations — `var(--t-hero)`, `var(--t-label)`, `var(--t-title)`, `var(--t-figure)`, `var(--t-lead)` — were **invalid at computed-value time**. The property inherited instead. The page rendered every text element at 13, 15 or 28px, the hierarchy it had been designed with never appeared, and **nothing failed**: a `var()` with no value is not an error, it is a declaration that quietly does nothing. The hero was set in the body face at weight 800 — the "fat ugly front" — because the rule that would have set the display face was the dead one.

Two rules follow, and they are not optional:

- **Every surface that renders type declares all eight names.** A surface may use six of them; it may not leave one undefined and reference it. A scale block that is missing a name is a trap, not a shortcut.
- **A token is a promise about a scope.** `--t-hero` means the same size everywhere it is defined, but it only means anything at all where it is defined. The scope is part of the token.

**A surface that writes its sizes as literals has no scale.** The rider app — the surface a rider actually holds — wrote **112 literal size declarations across 16 values**, and the sheet's expanded states alone carried 48/36/28/26/22/20/18/17/16/15/14/13/12/11. A surface that cannot name its own sizes cannot promise a count. The fix is the conversion, not the vigilance: the rider's scope now declares the eight names with the rider's values, every one of its declarations names a token, and **the count follows by construction** — the sheet's expanded state went from seven rendered sizes to three, and no future declaration can break the law without breaking the token rule first. (One declaration moved with it that is not the rider's: `.route-loading`, the Suspense fallback every route shows, was 12px — under the operations wall's own 13px floor — and is now `--t-label`.)

**Two sizes 2px apart with the same weight are one size.** The decision panel's verdict ("Expect delay", 34px, weight 200) and its countdown (36px, weight 200) were one treatment at two sizes: a 1.06× step, which is not a hierarchy, it is a coincidence. They share a size now. The panel has one voice at the top, and the reader gets one thing to look at.

**The rider's floor is 13px, because the rider is on a phone in sunlight.** The labels that say what a number means — "Next bus", "Live buses", "Temperature", "Bus occupancy", "Map", "More" — were 11px. The operations wall's floor is 13px and its screen is 50 inches across; the rider's phone is smaller than the wall and its labels are read outdoors. The rider surface's scale starts at 13px, the language toggle's two-letter codes went with it (its `min-width` is what sets the row's width, so the text grew without moving anything), and the desktop shell's chrome moved off 11px with them. **And it is a rule, not a number**: `typeaudit2.mjs` fails any element inside `.app-shell`, `.pa-shell` **or `.desktop-rider`** that owns text and renders under 13px, the same way it fails anything under the global 11px floor. A floor that lives only in a stylesheet is a number — and a floor whose SCOPE names only half the surfaces that use the tokens is half a floor. The desktop shell is a *sibling* of the app shell, not a child of it, so it was outside the rule that guards it.

**A scope must cover every surface that uses its tokens.** The desktop rider shell is a *sibling* of the app shell, and it renders rider type — so it declares the same eight values. A token that resolves against the root scope in a surface that means something else is a token carrying the wrong promise, and the wrong promise is invisible until someone reads the computed value.

**A ramp is measured at the width the rider holds.** `scripts/design/scalemap.mjs` takes a `WIDTH` env var, because a ramp that is legal at 1440 can be different at 390 — the desktop shell's chrome is gone at 390 and the app's own labels take its place. An instrument that can only measure the desktop reads a page the rider never sees, which is the same blindness as a half-loaded page.

`scripts/design/tokenscope.mjs` is the check: for every rule that names a token, the token must actually **resolve** on an element the rule matches. It found 17 dead declarations on the build that had the bug, and is clean on the build that does not. It has two passes that need no browser, and both run in the pre-commit hook: every `--t-*` name referenced must be defined somewhere, and **every scope that defines tokens must define all eight**. The second one is the law above, made mechanical — a scope with a hole in it is a trap. It found three: `:root` declared three of the eight names, `.v2--operations.fc` seven, `.v2--operations.study` seven. All three declare all eight now.

`scripts/design/offscale.mjs` is the other half: every size a page **renders** must be a size the scale **names**. It resolves tokens by *usage* — the sizes that token-ruled elements actually render at — so a fluid step is measured at the size it renders rather than at the floor of its `clamp()`. The first version of this probe parsed the token declarations instead, and cried wolf on the toolkit's 108px hero; an instrument that cries wolf is as useless as one that cannot fail.

**A green light on a check that checked nothing is the worst outcome a gate can have**, because it looks like evidence. Two of these probes were blind for a week, and both were blind in the same direction: they reported "clean" on pages that were wrong.

- **Chrome erases the declaration when it is written as a shorthand.** `font: 500 var(--t-hero)/1.05 var(--font-display)` is stored with every longhand carrying a pending substitution, so `rule.style.fontSize` reads `""`, `rule.style.font` reads `""`, and the property list shows `font-size = ` — present, empty. A rule that names a token looks like a rule that names no size at all. This codebase writes its type in the shorthand form everywhere, so both probes were blind to most of the stylesheet. The fix is not to re-implement the cascade — the browser does that correctly. The fix is to read the **signal**: a rule that lists a `font-size` property whose value reads empty is a rule whose size came from a `var()`. And where the token's *name* is needed, read the stylesheet **text** (`scripts/design/cssrules.mjs`), because that is the only place the name still exists.
- **A selector LIST is not one selector.** `button, input, a` is three selectors of specificity (0,0,1) each. Reading it as one selector and summing its type counts made it look stronger than it is, which changed which rule won, and so which leak was found.
- **A gate must pass its arguments as arguments.** The gate passed the routes to `offscale.mjs` as `--fleet/raw --fleet --study` — flags — and the probe drops anything that starts with `--`. It received zero routes, printed "total off-scale elements: 0", and the gate printed "ok" for a check that had never run.

**The law is enforced, not just recorded.** `scripts/design/typeaudit2.mjs` fails a page that renders more than eight sizes, at 1440 and at 390.

**The exception is closed (2026-10-21).** The rider phone app was the one named, dated exception, and it was the worst offender in the product: **18 sizes at 390**, from 26 distinct `font-size` declarations in `PassengerApp.css` written in `rem` against a 16px root. The screen a person holds in daylight carried microcopy at 9.9px and 10px, and its reading band was a fog of 12.5 / 13.1 / 13.6 / 14.1 / 14.7 / 15.2 — six sizes that are visually one size, which is the yellow-pages flatness this law exists to stop, only sideways.

The sheet now declares the eight names once, on the two roots that can carry them (`.pa-app` for the phone, `.pa-shell` for the portal chrome beside it), and every one of its 83 declarations is one of them. Measured on the rider domain at 390: **18 → 7**.

Three things the pass earned, worth keeping:

- **The reading band is ONE step on purpose.** A standfirst and a paragraph are the same size on a phone in sunlight; a 1px step between them is a fake hierarchy. The standfirst and the body are both 17, the labels are 13, and the smallest text on the screen is 11.
- **A third party can push a surface over the law.** Leaflet ships its own 18px zoom glyph and 12px attribution and tooltip. Neither was the app's, and neither was on the scale — the surface had to name them, or the count would drift back up with no one knowing which size did it.
- **A surface that renders type declares all eight names, on every root that can carry them.** The phone app renders *without* `.pa-shell` (the router returns the app content directly), so a scale declared on `.pa-shell` alone would have been undefined on the phone — the dead-rule trap, in a new shape.

With the exception closed, the switch that turned it off went with it: a switch with nothing to switch is a trap, and a named exception that is closed is a deleted law. The check still bites on any surface that crosses eight.

## Layout

- The map is the operating table. It takes the largest share of the first screen and nothing covers it on load. Everything else is an instrument docked at its edge, sized to its content.
- **Empty space is a decision**, never a filler. If a block could be removed without losing meaning, it is removed.
- No box, border, shadow, radius, pill or badge unless it groups things that belong together or it encodes a state.
- **One page, one left edge.** A page made of stacked bands — a header, a source switcher, a sub-nav, a body — puts every band's **content** on the same line. The bands themselves may be full-bleed; their content may not. This shipped broken: the toolkit's header sat at 24px, its body at 104px, and the top of the page looked like it had been built by a different team than the page under it. The console now has ONE column (`--v2-column`, `--v2-gutter`) and every band reads it; the dark operations wall is a different object and sets `--v2-column: 100%`, so one rule serves both views and they cannot drift apart. `scripts/design/guttercheck.mjs` measures the bands' content edges and fails when they disagree — it reported 80px of disagreement on the build that had the bug, and passes on the build that does not. **A control cluster that shares a row with the band before it is part of that band**, not a band of its own: the LIVE ⇄ SIMULATION switch sits at the top right of the operations header, so its left edge is not the page's gutter. It may sit anywhere to the right of the gutter, and it may never sit to the left of it.
- **A box that holds one sentence is a box drawn to fill a gap.** Eight bordered cards, each carrying a short quote, is a yellow pages, not a system. An index you can click and one panel that answers is half the boxes and a page you can use. The same rule kills the dead space under short cards: a grid that stretches every cell to the tallest one is padding the short ones with emptiness.
- **A grid track must be allowed to shrink.** `1fr` is `minmax(auto, 1fr)`, and its automatic minimum is the cell's min-content. Four cells whose text cannot break therefore add up to a track wider than the phone, and everything beside them is pushed off the screen: the toolkit's HUD pipeline went from two columns to four with no responsive override, and on every phone ≤441px the hero's prose was clipped at the right edge — the reader could not read the last characters of every line, and nothing said so. The tracks are `minmax(0, 1fr)`, the cells carry `min-width: 0`, and the pipeline is two columns on a phone. Eleven grids in the toolkit region had the same trap. **A grid is not a layout until it has been measured at 390.**
- **The type ramp is closed on every surface, the rider's phone included.** **Measured 2026-10-22 on the DEPLOYED site, 1440 and 390**, with `scripts/design/typeaudit2.mjs` (0 band violations on every route at both widths, and no text below the 11px floor on any route at either width): at 1440 — `/research` 3 · `/info` `/more` `/stops` `/ride` `/compare` 4 · `/study` 5 · `/` `/driver` 6 · `/ops` `/fleet` `/roi` `/v2` `/pass` 7 · `/fleet/raw` `/capacity` `/toolkit` `/governor` 8. A live page can also render one more size than the number above when the feed returns a state that draws an extra element, so `/fleet` reads 9 or 10. And the 390 pass is not optional: four toolkit headings were legal at 1440 and blobs at 390. The rule is ≤8.

- **One radius for the product.** The house radius is 0 (`--radius-sm/-md/-lg/-pill` are all 0). Full round is reserved for true circles and pills (`50%`, `999px`). One shape keeps a corner radius of its own: `20px 20px 0 0`, a photo with two rounded top corners. Nothing else gets a radius.

## Spatial hallucination

**A layout that invents a reason for things to occupy space, instead of arranging them around how people read, choose and act.** It looks composed at first glance and cannot explain its own geometry. The recurring test is: **"Why is this here, why this size, and why this far from that?"** If the answer is "the grid put it there", the layout has failed. Whitespace is intentional when it separates meaningful groups, supports reading, or establishes emphasis. It becomes hallucination when it asks the reader to supply an intention the designer never had.

Six failure modes, and every one of them is measurable:

1. **Unjustified gaps** — space stretched to fill a container. A grid stretches its cells to the tallest one; the short cell then carries a cushion of nothing.
2. **Orphan elements** — an element stranded in a mostly-empty row. A photo beside an empty area; a 60px span alone in a 588px row.
3. **Scattered actions** — related buttons on different rows, or pushed to opposite edges of a row that is mostly empty.
4. **False hierarchy** — everything gets equal weight, so nothing helps the reader choose. A grid of four or more identical boxed cells that owns half the screen.
5. **Accidental wrapping** — a row breaks where it runs out of room and strands one item alone on the last line.
6. **Spatially expensive information** — a small decision requires a long scroll. A region inside the page that is several screens deep.

**`scripts/design/space.mjs` measures all six, plus content clipped by its own box and scroll traps. It is a screen, not a judge: it reports geometry with its measurements, and a human decides whether the geometry is a decision.**

```bash
node scripts/design/space.mjs "/v2?view=toolkit" /ops --base http://127.0.0.1:4173
```

**It is proven to fail, and proven to be quiet.** `scripts/design/prove-space.sh` serves a fixture carrying one deliberate instance of every mode beside a clean control, and fails if a mode goes uncaught or the control goes noisy. The fixture found four real blindnesses in the instrument itself: a rule that skipped every grid whose children were explicitly placed (so a row that ended with a stranded item went unseen), a rule that compared every pair of actions in a row (so a navigation row spread across the width read as scattered actions), a dead-space rule that judged the page's own body, and an equal-weight rule that fired on every metadata row. A mutation test — blinding one rule — is what proves the proof itself bites.

**The instrument's own false positives are recorded here, because a gate that cries wolf is as useless as one that cannot fail:**

- A ROW is a line of items, not a set of identical tops. A pill is vertically centred and a chip beside it is not; a 3px difference once split one flex line into two, so a pill next to its own chips read as "alone on the last line". Rows are clustered with a tolerance.
- The page's OWN scroll is not a trap. A long briefing on a phone is a long briefing. A region INSIDE the page that is several screens deep is the trap.
- A wide DATA TABLE on a phone is a legitimate horizontal scroll. The map engine sizes its own overlay to the route's bounds, not to the viewport; both are exempt.
- A strip of three parallel facts is not a choice. The reader reads all of it and equal weight is the point. Only a grid of four or more identical cells that owns a large share of the screen is a false hierarchy.
- The page's TAIL is not a hole. Content that ends in the middle of the last screen is the end of the page.
- A cell's mark is the SET of colours it carries, not the colour of its first line. A green figure under a grey label marks the cell as surely as a tinted ground would.
- **A COLUMN stack is not a row that broke.** Every child of a column flex is on its own line by design, and `flex-wrap: wrap` in a column wraps on the HEIGHT. The live fleet table's route cell is exactly that — a line badge, a direction, a progress rule — and reading its last line as a widow fired on every live row of the operations wall: **81 of the 83 findings** the first deployed gate run brought back. The rule reads the direction before it reads the wrap, and the clean control now carries a column stack of three, which must report nothing.
- **A control cluster that shares a row with the band before it is not a band — but only while it is still in that band.** The operations wall puts its LIVE ⇄ SIMULATION switch at the top RIGHT of the header, and the toolkit's source switcher joined it there in the same row. Measured as a band, that group's left edge (910px) "disagreed" with the page's gutter (104px) — but it was never a band. It is part of the header strip, and the header strip's left edge is the brand's. A cluster may sit anywhere to the **right** of the gutter and is not measured against it; it may never sit to the **left** of it, which is the defect the rule was written for (the source switcher flush to the corner while the body sat at 104px). **The exemption is bounded by the band, because geometry may only make a rule stricter, never more lenient.** A declared cluster that has LEFT its band — moved onto a row of its own — is measured like a band, and a 400px drift cannot ride on a declaration that no longer describes the page. A cluster may never hang past the right edge of the screen. **A surface that declares no band FAILS**: a rule with nothing to measure must not print a pass. `scripts/design/prove-gutter.sh` drives nine cases against a fixture: the clean control passes; a band 80px off fails; a cluster pushed left of the gutter fails; a band dragged 400px right while sharing the header's row fails; a cluster that has left its band fails; the insights bridge — where the switcher IS a band on its own row — passes when it sits on the gutter and fails when it does not; a cluster that hangs past the right edge of the screen fails; and a surface that declares no band at all fails. That last case is driven through the REAL instrument by handing it a surface table from a file, because no real surface has no band — and a rule that cannot be exercised cannot be proven.
- **Two bus dots on a rail are not two scattered actions — and only two bus dots.** The fleet console draws each bus on the corridor rail at `left: NN%` — its position is where the bus IS, not where the layout put it. Two buses 149px apart on a 258px rail is a gap in the DATA, and reading it as a gap in the layout made the gate fail or pass with the traffic: the same build, the same code, a different answer depending on which buses were moving. The exemption is **scoped to the two rail classes** (`.fc-line__bus`, `.fd-corridor-bus`) and it takes **both** members of the pair: one marker and one real action is still a pair to measure, and an action that merely happens to be absolutely positioned is still an action. The first version of this exemption was generic — "any absolutely positioned button placed by a percentage" — and it would have excused a real pair of scattered actions, which is the same defect as a rule that is too narrow, wearing the same coat.
- **DOM order is not visual order.** The space between two boxes is the space between them, in whichever order they sit. A button placed by a percentage can come FIRST in the markup and land on the RIGHT; a gap computed as `B.left - A.right` then reads as a large negative number and the pair is never measured at all. The proof caught this: the fixture's scattered pair was invisible to the instrument, and an instrument that cannot see a defect looks exactly like a clean page. The proof now also covers the pair the FIRST version of the marker exemption missed: two actions that are BOTH placed by a percentage and neither of which is a rail marker. The exemption takes **both** members or it takes none.

**The gate must run against the DEPLOYED site, and this is why it is written that way.** The local preview has no backend: it renders the SIMULATION fleet, so the live row's shape was never measured and the local site was clean at **0** while production carried **81 false findings and 2 real ones**. The two real ones were in the LIVE bar — the strip that replaces the timebar when real buses are reporting. Its basis ("Fleet, trips & km: GPS · Demand, queues and dispatch: model · Riders: …") was **624px of prose in a 342px strip**: the narrow block pins every timebar child to `flex: 0 0 auto`, which is right for a control and wrong for prose, so the sentence ran off the right edge of a phone — and because it filled its line by itself, the DEVICES action was pushed onto a line of its own below it. The basis and the action that belongs with it are now **one group**: they wrap as one unit, the basis gives way (it is prose; it ellipsises) and the action keeps its size. It had to be a markup change: flex wraps on the items' *hypothetical* sizes, so a shrinking basis and the action beside it are still placed on different lines. **The pair has to be one element before the break can happen where it should.**

- **A page waiting on a TIMER looks exactly like a page that has finished.** This is the blindness the shared settle (`scripts/design/settle.mjs`) was built to close, and then the blindness it reopened. The first version waited for the network to go quiet and for the document to hold still — and it **returned earlier than the fixed five-second wait it replaced**, because the legacy chain's first call 404s in 200ms, the network is quiet at ~1s, and the stop list is empty and stays empty until the retry lands at 3.2s. "The document stops changing" is precisely what a page waiting for a timer looks like. The settle now has a **floor**: it never returns before the wait it replaced, and the document-stability check runs *after* the floor, so a timer that fired while the gate waited is caught. `scripts/design/prove-settle.sh` proves it with the case that was missing — a fixture whose content arrives on a **timer**, with no request in flight — and it proves the floor is load-bearing by running the same page with the floor removed and showing the gate then measures it empty. The network fixture could not catch this: its request is in flight for seven seconds, so the network is never quiet and the settle waits correctly.

**Where it stands, 2026-10-23, measured on the deployed site:** `scripts/design/verify-design.sh https://bus.nonarkara.org` returns **all checks pass** on the 18 routes it measured then, and **the six routes it had never measured held 63 spatial findings and 10 elements past the right edge** — the four toolkit tabs and the two `/v2` views. The gate was green because it was measuring less than the site served. **Where it stands now, 2026-10-23, after the rider scale, the toolkit fixes and two rounds of review:** the gate measures **all 24 routes** at **both widths** and **all six checks pass** — tokens, one left edge, the scale, the edge, the ramp law, the geometry. `typeaudit2.mjs` returns **0 violations on all 48 measurements** (24 routes × 2 widths), nothing under the global 11px floor and nothing under the rider's 13px floor. The spatial instrument returns **0 findings** on all 48. The three proof suites pass on a quiet machine: **settle 4, ship 16, space 22** — and they run in CI now. **The number that matters is the one on the deployed site**: a local preview reads 0 because it has no backend to measure.

**What the massacre found, measured on the local build at 1440 and 390, 2026-10-21** — the operations wall's right rail was 527px tall and held 1,652px of content: the hour-by-hour analysis sat BETWEEN the headline and the fleet table, so the thing the rail exists for finished 1,482px below the fold and nothing on the surface said it was down there. The fleet table's height came from four rules written when the table sat under the map and the two divided a vertical column; the table moved into the rail, the rules stayed, and the height of the thing the rail exists for was the residue of their source order. The rail was 380px, which the note beside it already said was too narrow, so the ETA column was off the edge and the operator had to scroll a wall sideways to read it. A FULL badge shared the load cell with the bar and squeezed the bar to zero width, so every row's occupancy was invisible. On a phone, the briefing's second column wanted 320px, the RED FLAG banner was `width: max-content` — 627px of text in a 388px map — and the timebar's chips and controls together wanted 472px of a 340px row.

**The rules that came out of it, each one earned by a measurement:**

- **A rail's width is set by what the rail holds.** 380px starved both the fleet table's five tracks and the operating equation's three terms. The rail is now `clamp(460px, 34vw, 520px)`, and the map keeps 60%+ at every width.
- **A region's height is a decision, made where the region lives.** Rules left over from a layout that no longer exists will decide the geometry of the one that does, if nobody deletes them.
- **The first screen answers three questions, in order, and nothing else.** How much did we lose today, what do I do about it right now, which bus is it. Everything else is one bounded scroll away.
- **A bar that is squeezed to zero width is a measurement failure, not a design.** The badge next to it was the winner; the bar was the truth.
- **A cap must be applied to the thing that overflows, not to its container.** Capping a strip does not shorten its tiles.
- **A narrow-width rule that sits before the rule it overrides does nothing.** The declaration is there; it simply loses on source order. The narrow-width fixes live at the END of the stylesheet.
- **A peer set must have a leader.** Four or more identical boxed cells that own half the screen are a yellow pages, not a system. Three parallel facts in a row are not a choice, and equal weight is the point.
- **A record is a ledger, not a grid of boxes.** Seven equal bordered cells holding seven numbers is the shape this file's own rules call the commonest slop — and on a six-column grid the seventh cell stranded itself in a row with **1,170px of empty page** beside it. `/study`'s archive is now a ledger: a caption, the figure, and what the figure means, one row each, a hairline between them, the figures reading down a single axis. Nothing is boxed, so nothing can be a peer of anything else, and the phone gets the same ledger in three lines a row instead of seven cards — **1,022px of cards became 700px of record**. A modelled figure says so in its label, in its note AND in a quieter ink: a modelled number must never read like a counted one.
- **Five boxes holding five unrelated numbers are a row of boxes, not a status strip.** The decision panel's status row was five grey chips — one CSS rule, no variants, so the box carried no meaning — and at 440px the row overflowed and left one chip alone on the last line. The first fix treated the symptom (a two-column grid on phones, which is how the row used to break there). The strip is **one line of text separated by a middot** now: every fact kept, no box, no orphan, and it wraps as prose at any width. A row of status facts is a sentence, and a sentence does not need five pieces of furniture around it.
- **A count that rides beside its label makes the row too wide, and the row then breaks where it runs out of room.** `/fleet/raw`'s four filters were 373px of labels-plus-counts in a 358px phone row; the row broke 3 + 1 and left a chip alone on the last line. The count now sits **under** its name: the row is as wide as the four names (315px) and every filter stays on one line. The count is the one thing in a chip that can be lost when the label cannot.
- **A route that could not be measured is not a clean route.** The space instrument asked a dead browser context for a page, the context was dead too, and the instrument **died** — on `/research`, the route right after the one it was reading. A gate that stops checking is worse than one that reports: it looks like a pass. A dead context is replaced now, a dead browser is relaunched, and an unmeasured route is counted as a finding, never as a clean one. **And a route that is not there is not a clean route either**: the instrument measured whatever HTML came back, so a 404 — or a typo in the route list — read as a pass on a page nobody asked for. The status is the answer to "is this the route?". **And neither is a route that redirects somewhere else**: `goto` follows the redirect and reports the FINAL status, so a 302 that lands on the homepage answers 200 — and the gate would measure the homepage and call it the route it wanted. The instrument now compares the pathname it ASKED for with the pathname it LANDED on, and the proof carries a fixture server that redirects on purpose, because a rule that cannot be proven cannot be trusted.
- **An empty range is not a fleet that did nothing.** A sum over zero days is arithmetically 0, but it is not a measurement, and the page must never print a silent zero where the honest answer is "we saw nothing". `/study`'s archive had exactly that: anchor a date before the collector existed and it read "Tracked km 0 · Runs 0 · 0 h" — and then a caveat that announced "**These are town buses.** No tracked bus came within 1 km of HKT airport", asserting a fact about a fleet that was never tracked. The rows now read **no data**, the caveat says the range carries no sample, and the counter caveat says the same instead of claiming a verification over a set of zero days. The collector's own liveness is read from the **RANGE**, not from today: the old lookup asked for today's row, found none on a historical anchor, and cried "Not recording — check the cron" about a collector that had archived that day perfectly well. **A range that ended last week is a record, and a record does not go stale.**
- **The smallest text a reader is asked to read is 11px, and a floor that lives only in a stylesheet is a number.** The rider phone app's rule has said 11 since 2026-10-21; the legacy consumer chain was below it — 9px in three declarations, 10px in thirteen, rendering 10px bottom-nav tab labels on seven routes, a 10px air-quality line and a 10px ticket meta on a phone. The first attempt to fix it moved those declarations onto `var(--t-micro)`, and **the deployed build failed its own gate**: the offscale instrument judges a surface once half its text is token-ruled, and the legacy chain had literals everywhere else — a surface that is HALF on tokens has a scale the instrument cannot name, so every remaining literal (12px, 15px, 22px, 24px, 34px) came off it. **The legacy chain is on literal sizes by design, and a half-token surface is a broken surface.** So the floor is 11px literal, and `scripts/design/typeaudit2.mjs` now FAILS any element that owns text and renders below 11px: a floor the audit checks is a rule, and a floor in a stylesheet is a number.
- **A rule that cannot tell a band from a cluster by its geometry will excuse one and fail the other.** The first version of the control-cluster carve-out decided a probe's role from where it sat: "shares a row with the band before it" means cluster. So a band that happened to share a row with another probe was excused, and a cluster that dropped to a second row was reported as a band and failed on a defect that did not exist. The role is now **declared** per surface — a probe is a band or a cluster, and a band is measured against the gutter wherever it sits vertically. It also ends the zero-margin comparison the guess depended on: the toolkit's source switcher sits 0.0px from the brand's centre, and the rule above it was written to tolerate 12px.

## Shipability

**The layout gate asks whether the page is right. The shipability audit asks whether the thing can be handed over.** A site can pass every layout check and still throw on every page, report every visitor's IP address to a third party, call twenty-two endpoints that do not exist, and keep a live admin token in its git history. Every one of those has happened here. `scripts/design/shipaudit.mjs` is the instrument; `scripts/design/prove-ship.sh` proves it bites; it runs on **every deploy** from `scripts/verify-deploy.sh`.

- **A tracker that records nothing and still leaks is worse than one that is honestly described.** The visitor tracker sent one ping per session — IP, city, country, region, hostname, page, referrer, user agent, language, screen, timezone — to a personal Google Sheet, after resolving the IP through **ipapi.co**. The disclosure described it accurately and appeared on **two routes out of seventeen**. Then the sheet was locked: the pings stopped recording. The IP lookup did not. Every visitor's address still left their browser, on every route, to a third party, to feed a sheet that could not receive it. It is **removed** — the tracker, the notice's old text, and `script.google.com` and `ipapi.co` from the CSP's `connect-src`. The notice now says what is true: Cloudflare's own first-party beacon, nothing else.
- **The audit must not be the thing that leaks.** The instrument **aborts** every request to a known collector host rather than making it, so running the audit on the live site cannot send a reader's address to the place the audit is complaining about — and so the proof is hermetic.
- **A disclosure belongs on the route the reader is ON.** A notice on the route nobody visits is not a notice. The instrument measures the actual outbound request, and then the actual page text on that same route.
- **A probe that 404s is a dead endpoint.** The static site has no Express backend, but `USE_LIVE` was hard-coded true, so every call tried the live API first and fell back — **22 round-trips that could not succeed, on every route**, before the real data appeared, each one a red line in the console. **176 dead-endpoint calls across 16 routes.** The answer to "is there a backend?" is now asked **once per session**, at `/api/capabilities`, which answers **200 with `{backend: false}`** on the static deployment and `{backend: true}` from the Express server. The swap is a property of the **deployment**, not a constant in the source — and a probe that asks its question of an endpoint that **exists** leaves no trace, while a probe that asks it of one that does not is itself a dead endpoint.
- **An upstream that refuses everyone is not an outage of this site.** `api.airplanes.live` is invitation-only now (403, "contact us… your email MUST include a description of the project"). The browser called it directly and CORS blocked it, so **every page that draws the operations map printed a red CORS error** while the overlay quietly showed its schedule beads anyway. The edge now asks once (`/api/adsb`) and always answers **200** — empty with `status: "unavailable"` — so the overlay degrades the same way, with nothing broken and nothing red. If the upstream ever grants access, the overlay comes alive with no change to the client.
- **A secret that was removed is not removed.** The PKSB bearer token is in this repo's **public** history — `server/config.ts`, added 2026-03-08, removed 2026-04-07 — as a JWT that does not expire until **2035**. The instrument scans the tree AND the history. The fix that removes the risk is **rotation at PKSB**; rewriting history is hygiene.
- **A finding that no deploy can fix must not block every deploy.** The history scan found the token, and the first version of the audit made it a *finding* — so the deploy gate was red on the commit that shipped it, forever, on a condition no deploy can change. A gate that is permanently red is a gate that gets switched off, and when it is switched off the checks that CAN be fixed stop running with it. A secret in a published commit is already public: what clears it is a rotation at the issuer and a rewrite of the history, and both are the owner's decisions. So it is now a **CARRIED DEBT** — reported in its own block on every run, with the steps that clear it, and counted in the verdict line: *"shippable — 0 blocking findings, 1 carried debt. A carried debt is not a pass; it is a thing that is still owed."* A secret in the **working tree** is still a finding, and it still blocks.
- **A gate's own escape hatch must announce itself.** `SKIP_SHIPABILITY=1` used to skip the audit in silence. A hatch that says nothing is a broken gate, not a hatch; it now prints, in the deploy log, every time it is used.
- **The notice must be keepable.** The rewritten privacy notice said *"nothing about you is sent anywhere else"* — and the map tiles come from OpenStreetMap and the weather from GISTDA and RainViewer, so those services see the reader's request, and so their IP address, the way every website they visit does. A notice that cannot be kept is not a notice; it is a promise the site will break on the first map tile. The text now names the three services and says what they see. **The same sentence also said the bus positions are "computed in your browser", which is false in LIVE mode** — there they are relayed from the public PKSB tracker by this site's own server, which is the better fact and the one worth saying.
- **TWO FAILURES, TWO EXIT CODES.** The deploy script has exactly two things it can say, and the difference between them changes what the operator does next. `verify-deploy.sh` exits **2** when the BYTES did not reach the host — the build is missing, the live asset graph is not the local build, the production deployment carries another commit, the edge function is not answering, the two hosts disagree — and **1** when the GATE failed. The first version of the split printed *"the bytes ARE deployed and live — the upload worked"* on **any** failure, so a deploy that never arrived was dressed as one that did, and the site stayed on the old build while the log said it shipped. A message that cannot be false is worth two exit codes.
- **A THIRD PARTY'S REQUEST CARRIES THE READER'S IP ADDRESS — SO THE FIRST FIX IS TO STOP ASKING.** The instrument now records every request the page makes to a service outside this site, and it asks the obvious question afterwards: does the page SAY so? Before that check existed, the disclosure rule could never fire for the hosts the site really contacts, which is why a false promise survived until a human read it. Adding the check found something better than a missing notice: `src/engine/dataProvider.ts` fetched a **RainViewer** radar timestamp **at module load**, so the tourist app, the study, the research pages and the rider's phone each reported their reader to a weather service for a tile layer that nothing in the client renders. **21 route-and-service pairs on 19 routes** became **2** — `/` and `/driver`, both GISTDA's PM2.5 — the moment the fetch moved to where the layer is built. The fix was not a longer privacy notice; it was to stop asking.
- **A THIRD PARTY IS NOT A COLLECTOR, AND THE INSTRUMENT MUST NOT ABORT IT.** The collector list aborts the request, so the audit cannot send a reader's address to the very service it is checking for. A map tile is not a collector: aborting it would change the measurement of every page that draws a map. So the third parties have their own list, they are **not** aborted, and the check asks a different question — does the page NAME the service? The disclosure sits where the third party's data enters the page: **"Air quality GISTDA · map tiles OpenStreetMap — both see your request."**, one line, no box, under the air-quality badge it explains.
- **ONLY THE PUBLISHED COMMIT IS DEBT. A SECRET IN THE TREE ALWAYS BLOCKS.** The debt routing tested `where.includes("history")` — a SUBSTRING test. A working-tree secret in a file called `history/leaked.txt` was therefore reported as carried debt, and debt does not block a deploy. The repo has tracked files under `public/toolkit/history/`, so this was not a hypothetical directory name. The test is now an equality against the one place the history is scanned, and `scripts/design/prove-ship.sh` has the case that bites: the same secret under a path that mentions history, and it must block.
- **THE PROOF'S OWN FIXTURES ARE EXEMPT FROM BOTH SCANS.** The secret fixtures hold a JWT on purpose. The tree scan exempts them by their full path, and the history scan now exempts them the same way — reading the diff's own file header to know which file an added line belongs to. Without that, the proof's test data added a debt entry to **every** deploy, mixed in with the real one, in the one block a human has to read carefully.
- **A ROUTE MATCHED WITH `===` IS A ROUTE.** The coverage derivation reads the app's source for the routes it serves, and it read only `startsWith("…")`. A route declared as `pathname === "/newroute"` — which is how the app matches `/` — was invisible to it, and a route nobody measures is the whole failure the check exists for. It reads both ways now.
- **A PROOF NOBODY RUNS IS NOT A PROOF.** The three instrument suites — `prove-settle.sh`, `prove-ship.sh`, `prove-space.sh` — are the only thing standing between a green light and a check that has quietly stopped checking anything. They ran on one machine, on one day, in one session. **They run in CI now**, with the browser installed by the workflow rather than assumed, because a rule checked only when a human remembers to check it is a number and not a rule.
- **A CONSTANT A COMPONENT'S CONTENT CAN INVALIDATE IS A NUMBER PRETENDING TO BE A LAYOUT.** The language toggle, the weather pill, the live badge and Leaflet's own top-right controls all sat at `top: var(--hero-below, 132px)` — and `--hero-below` was never set by anything. The 132px was a constant calibrated for three stacked blocks in the hero (next-bus, safety strip, PM2.5 badge), and the hero has since grown a fourth. The toggle painted OVER the new line; the pill and the badge disappeared BEHIND it. The stylesheet's comment already promised the variable was set from the visible hero size; **it is now — by a `ResizeObserver` on the hero, republished every time its content changes**, because the safety strip comes and goes with the data and the PM2.5 badge only exists once GISTDA answers.
- **A THIRD PARTY THE LIST HAS NEVER HEARD OF IS STILL A THIRD PARTY.** The governor's page embeds a Microsoft Power BI document — a frame that carries the reader's address to `app.powerbi.com` — and the page named the DATA (ThaiRSC) without naming the SERVICE. The list now includes it, the page names it, and the rule is stated honestly: the list cannot be exhaustive, so what keeps it honest is that **the audit records every contact the page makes and prints it**, which means a contact the list does not know still shows up in the log as a service this site has not accounted for. A check that prints nothing when it passes cannot be re-derived from the log — a run that saw two contacts and a run that saw none read the same.
- **AN UNHANDLED FAILURE IS A BYTES FAILURE, NOT A GATE FAILURE.** The deploy's contract is "2 = the bytes did not reach the host, 1 = the gate failed", and every explicit check says which it is. But a `sort` that errored, a `git rev-parse` outside a repository or a curl that could not connect ended the script with **1** — which means "the gate failed" — so the deploy printed *"the bytes ARE deployed and live"* after a check that never finished. An `ERR` trap makes the implicit paths explicit, the commit lookup degrades to "byte check only" instead of stopping the run, and the deployment list is a cross-check rather than a gate.
- **THE BYTES ARE NOT LIVE *YET* IS NOT THE SAME AS THE BYTES ARE NOT LIVE.** The upload and the verification are seconds apart, and Cloudflare's edge takes a little longer than that to serve the new deployment on the published domain. The check ran while the alias was still serving the PREVIOUS deployment's chunks, reported "the live host does not serve them", and the deploy said **the upload had failed** — for a deployment that had landed and was live a minute later. The two facts are different, and only one of them is a failure: so the byte check now retries for a couple of minutes before it says anything, and what it says afterwards is which of the two it found. A gate that reports a half-propagated edge as a broken upload sends the operator to fix the wrong thing.
- **A value belongs to the name on its OWN LINE.** The secret pattern once allowed a newline between the name and the value, so `SMARTBUS_BEARER_TOKEN=` on one line matched the **next line's name** as its value, and an example file with no secrets in it was reported as a leak. A newline is a separator, not whitespace inside a value.
- **The gate must measure every route the app serves.** The layout gate covered **18 of 24** routes; the six it missed held 63 spatial findings and 10 elements past the right edge. The instrument now derives the served routes from the CODE (`src/App.tsx`) and from `public/_redirects`, and requires that both appear in the gate's own list — so a route added to one and forgotten in the other is a finding, not a surprise.

- **A SIZE THE AUTHOR SET IS A DECISION; ONLY A SIZE THE GRID CHOSE IS A LEFTOVER.** The pain map draws each country's score as a red bar whose height IS the score, and the instrument read the space under the "5/5" label as 140px of dead space — on five bars, the DATA, called waste. A cell with an explicit inline height is sized on purpose. The same argument covers the finance rows, whose fill width IS the amount: a 46% cash bar in a 292px row is a measurement, and the space beside it is the rest of the money, not an item stranded in a row of its own.
- **THE LAST LINE OF A BOX IS A RANGE OVER ITS TEXT, NOT THE BOTTOM OF ITS LAST ELEMENT.** The credit-memo paragraphs open with an inline `<strong>` and carry the rest as plain text, so the last ELEMENT inside them sat on the first line — and the two lines after it were measured as 54px and 80px of dead space under paragraphs that had none.
- **`overflow: hidden` CLIPS AT THE PADDING BOX, NOT THE CONTENT BOX.** The debt-service chart's dashed "lender gate" line deliberately overhangs the three columns by a few percent, which put it inside the container's 10% horizontal padding — 75px "past the edge" of a box it was never cut by. The limit is where the content actually disappears.
- **A PAGE MEASURED AT TWO WIDTHS IS TWO PAGES.** `goto` to the same URL with a different fragment is a SAME-DOCUMENT navigation: nothing reloads, nothing re-measures, and a page that fitted itself to the viewport at mount keeps the old fit at the new width. The toolkit's system map fits its bounds once, on mount — so at 390 the instrument was measuring a 1440-projected map squeezed into a 348px box, and reported a tooltip 8px past an edge it had never been near. The instrument resets the document before a hash route; the map re-fits on resize, because a phone turned sideways is a real thing that happens.
- **A PHONE IS A LEDGER, NOT A STACK OF BOXES.** Six framework tests, five delivery phases, five source links, five research cards, eight lessons and five contract cards were bordered cells each on a phone — and identical cells are PEERS: nothing in them tells the reader where to look. The repo's own rule is "space or one hairline between items over a box around each", so on a phone the grid gives up its boxes and becomes a ledger: one rule between the rows, nothing around them. The desktop keeps its columns, where they are columns.
- **A SPECIFICITY BEATS A LATER RULE.** The dark sections gave their research cards a ground of their own with a compound selector, and the phone ledger's plain rule could not take it back — the cards stayed boxes and the finding stayed. The phone rule has to NAME the compound selectors it is undoing.
- **A PAGE THAT STATES ITS ANSWER WHERE THE READER IS NOT LOOKING IS A PAGE WITHOUT AN ANSWER.** Four contract cards that all read the same, with the recommendation written in a sentence above the grid, is a peer set with no leader. The card now carries it — the house treatment for an answer is the yellow ground, and the badge says it in words as well as colour.
- **A PERMANENT TOOLTIP OPENS TO THE RIGHT OF ITS MARKER, UNLESS THE MARKER SAYS OTHERWISE.** On a 350px phone map, a marker near the right edge pushed its label off the canvas. The map knows where the marker is; the direction follows it.

- **A PAGE MEASURED BEFORE IT HAS FINISHED LOADING IS NOT THE PAGE.** The gate waited a fixed number of seconds — five for most checks, **two and a half** for the type audit — and called that a page. The legacy consumer chain asks the server for its stops, gets a 404, waits three seconds and asks again, so at two and a half seconds the stop list can still be EMPTY. Every instrument now waits on two signals — **the network goes quiet** (a page still waiting for data is not quiet; the cap is there because a live ticker never goes quiet) and **the document stops changing** (a quiet network is not a finished page — content can arrive from a timer) — **and on a floor of five seconds**, because the first version of that wait returned EARLIER than the fixed wait it replaced: a page waiting on a timer is perfectly stable. `scripts/design/prove-settle.sh` proves the floor is load-bearing by measuring a fixture whose content arrives on a timer with no request in flight, and then measuring the SAME fixture with the floor removed.

    | `settle` (floor 5s) | 6,532ms | 6,426px | 704 | 52 |

    The page is **6,426px at every wait from two seconds to thirty**, and the stop list is **full — 52 cards — in every one of them**. - **A CELL THAT SPANS TWO ROWS IS NOT A ROW, AND THE INSTRUMENT MUST NOT PRETEND OTHERWISE.** The spatial gate bands a grid's items by vertical overlap, so an item that spans two rows overlaps the item below it and the two rows become ONE band. That is right when the spanning cell fills the second row's first column — the reader sees no stranded item — and wrong when it does not. The threshold is defensible; the case was **untested**, and an untested case is a rule nobody can rely on. It is tested now, as an A/B: `space-tall-item.html` (the first cell spans) must report **nothing**, and `space-tall-item-no-span.html` (the same three items, the same grid, the first cell not tall) must report the **orphan**. Only the span differs between the two files, so only the span can decide the verdict — a proof where both reported the same thing would prove nothing.
- **A SCROLLER THAT FILLS ITS OWN SCROLL CONTAINER IS THAT CONTAINER'S SCROLL.** The rider app is a 440px phone frame on a desktop; the frame's app-content is 849px tall and the stops list inside it is 809px — the list IS the app's page, and it missed the viewport test by **one pixel** (809 against an 810 threshold). The nearest scrollable ancestor is the right yardstick: a scroller that fills 90% of the thing that scrolls it is not a hole inside it. The operations rail is 59% of the viewport — a region beside the map — and stays a trap.
- **A TRACK THAT SHRINKS TO NOTHING IS NOT A TRACK.** On a desktop the rider app is a 440px phone mockup, and the two-column stops rule was written for the 1000px shell the same media query sets up. Applied to the frame it left the stop list **108px wide** and cut every stop card's name, time and map link off at the edge — 12 clipped elements on one route. A track must be allowed to shrink; a track that shrinks to 108px and clips its content has not shrunk, it has disappeared.

- **`public/toolkit-source.pdf` is 5.8 MB.** It is a document behind an explicit "Download the toolkit" link, not page weight, and it is the source document the research cites. It is the one heavy file left in `public/` and it is there on purpose.
- **The research photographs live in `assets-src/`, not `public/`.** They are the evidence base and they are versioned in the repository; they are not page assets. Shipping 44 MB of unreferenced photographs to the open web was the single largest piece of dead weight this branch carried.
- **The live half and the modelled half never share a label.** The corner ticker reads the real fleet from the operator's tracker in the "Live · PKSB tracker" block and this page's own model in the "Simulated · this page's model" block. Before 2026-10-20 the badge showed only the model under a "Live" kicker, which is a lie a reader can act on.

## What the branch is, operationally

- The toolkit deploys as a **preview deployment** of the `phuket-smart-bus` Pages project. `bus.nonarkara.org` is the **production branch** of the same project. The two never touch: `scripts/deploy-usdot.sh` deploys with `--branch=depa-usdot.nonarkara.org` and cannot overwrite production.
- **Pages Functions only run on the production branch.** So `cloudflare/toolkit-domain-router.ts` — the Worker that holds the `depa-usdot.nonarkara.org` custom domain — routes `/api/*` to the production deployment and everything else to the toolkit's preview deployment. That split is why the published domain can serve the real tracker relay and the research archive at all.
- **Test-data injection does not exist in a production build.** The DEVICES console's "Inject Live GPS Pulse" fabricates positions for real PKSB plate numbers; on a dev machine that is a fixture, on a published wall it is a lie. It is behind `import.meta.env.DEV`.
- The source register (`src/components/toolkit/DataProvenance.tsx`) carries a **"Live"** freshness value that means the operator's tracker is reporting it now. A published timetable is **"Published"**, not "Live" — the legend says so, and so does the register.
